# Question bank (ask 3 to 4 at a time; offer tap options plus "Other, I'll type it")

Good questions ask about ONE moment. Bad questions ask about a category and get a summary back.

## Setup
- "What should I call you, and how do you want to use me: chat only, or with your own browser?"
- "How much should I do on your account? Draft only / ask before every action / likes on my own, ask for the rest."
- "How much time can you give this per week? 1 hour / 2 to 3 hours / 4+ hours."
- "What must never appear in your posts? (employer names, salary, family, politics, religion, health, conflicts, other)"

## The life you want (3 to 5 years)
- "Picture a normal Tuesday in 3 years. What are you doing, and who are you doing it with?"
- "What matters most in the next job: growth, money, flexibility, people, purpose? Pick two."
- "What hours, place and pace feel right? (remote / hybrid / office; full-time / part-time)"
- "What money range would feel like enough? You can keep this private; I will not post it."

## The work you want
- "Which 2 or 3 job titles would make you proud to write on your profile?"
- "Which kind of company: startup, agency, big company, nonprofit, your own thing?"
- "What did you do in a past job that you never want to do again?"
- "Which part of work makes you lose track of time?"

## Strengths with proof (one moment each)
- "Tell me about a time you fixed something nobody asked you to fix."
- "What are you the person people come to for?"
- "What is the best result you helped create? Give a number, even rough, and say it is rough."
- "What did you build, write, organize or improve that still exists?"
- "Which part was actually yours, not the team's?"

## Where you are now
- "What is your current role (or last role), and how long?"
- "What is missing from your CV that the jobs you want ask for?"
- "Do you have recommendations, projects, certificates or writing I can use?"

## Turning points and scars (ask once, accept the answer)
- "What did you believe a year ago that you no longer believe?"
- "What mistake taught you the most?"
- "What do you check now that you never used to?"

## Voice
- "Pick 3 words for how you sound: warm, direct, funny, calm, bold, curious, formal, simple."
- "Name a person on LinkedIn whose posts you like. What do you like about how they write?"
- "Words you love. Words you refuse to use."

## Audience
- "Who should notice you first: recruiters, hiring managers, people in your field, future clients?"
- "What do they worry about that you can help with?"

## Naming and limits
- "Can I name your employer or past employers in posts, or keep them general?"
- "Who would be unhappy to see themselves mentioned?"
- "Anything under NDA or still in progress?"

## Post-mode interview (one topic, 5 to 8 questions)
1. "When did this last actually happen to you?"
2. "Walk me through it. What happened first?"
3. "What is the number in this story?"
4. "What did you get wrong at the time?"
5. "Who would disagree with your view?"
6. "What should a reader do differently tomorrow?"
7. Read the spine back in five lines. Their correction is usually the best line: keep it word for word.

## Questions that waste time
"Tell me about yourself", "What are your strengths?", "What makes you unique?", "What are your content pillars?" They get adjectives and marketing words. Ask for moments instead.
